### 1.2 VOID, and what it costs

- BEFORE CODE_PIN_9 the file is VOID when CODE_PIN_9 is not tagged by the close of the 10th NYSE session after T_F (and
  T_F is not pushed when G2b fails before it). No price-bearing request of section 3.1 exists before CODE_PIN_9, so no
  fit by the job can exist then; the option to choose WHEN a file is registered is every file's (section 12).
- AFTER CODE_PIN_9 THE ONLY VOID is a failure of G1, G2, G2b or G3 recorded in a VOID receipt that the job pushes itself
  (section 1.1 step e) with its inputs' hashes. A G1 failure is one only when every request of section 3.1 ended with
  status 200 or a documented 'not found' (the manifest's final status counts are in the receipt); a G1 failure with a
  server error, a timeout or a network error among its final statuses draws as a lapse does.
- WHAT A VOID COSTS: nothing in the lane's budget. A VOID version registers nothing, never decides, never seats, is never
  judged and draws no charge (the 2 x c9 RESERVED at T_F, section 10, is released). Its id may be registered again only as
  a new version with its own four times. There is no limit on VOIDs and no third-registration draw: a VOID is reached only
  by a mechanical gate of pinned code over hashed inputs that anyone with the vault can recompute, so it carries no
  option on an outcome.
- EVERY OTHER PATH AFTER CODE_PIN_9 DRAWS 2 x c9 (NO ABANDON OPTION; re-check 1's major 2, re-check 2's major 3): no
  TRAIN-START on GitHub by the close of the 5th NYSE session after CODE_PIN_9; no receipt on GitHub by the close of the
  5th NYSE session after TRAIN-START; a G4 or G5 failure; a stop after TRAIN-START because a hash differs. Such a
  version never decides or seats, and its id's next version is a new design (section 1.3). A person may re-run the job
  or push the job's own receipt commit unchanged, and nothing else.
- After T_M nothing voids a version. A fault found later holds its real money (section 7.5) and is fixed only by a new
  version.

  result.

### 2.3 Candidates at tau

- For each pool program p, in pool order, the events p's own rule takes for D as real money reads them:

  event of the first of them in pool order, and the learner's intent, if picked, is that event. An event with no CIK is
  no candidate.
- Its FEATURE EVENT is the event that its primary pool program's own selection takes for the issuer with every verdict
  read as missing: `programs.select` with `verdict` returning None, which for every pool program is the issuer's
  earliest knowable event of that program's rows (then the lowest event id). Every column but `multi`, the primary
  dummies and `first20` reads only the feature event; those three read live membership, which verdicts move through
  PREREG-EVL-5's TAKEN (b) and WAIT and the 8-K guard of `book.picks` (section 3.5's stated skew).
- KEPT (the fit's centring): TRADABLE at decision.
- ELIGIBLE TO BE PICKED: kept, and (i) its primary event is knowable after COUNT_FROM_9 of the version; (ii) THE
  20-SESSION SPACING (re-check 1's blocker 1, its fix (c)): the learner holds no written, non-skipped intent of the issuer
  entering in the 20 sessions before D, nor in the 20 sessions after D (PREREG-EVL-1 section 5's one position per
  issuer, as A72 reads it, with 20 sessions in place of 5 for this file's ids), counting every written, non-skipped
  intent of the learner's id and version, the catch-up's and a failed part's later decision's included, so a session's
  picks are the same however it was decided. (A pick of an issuer the learner picked a few sessions earlier is the
  channel through which a persistent issuer effect reaches a run's blocks again and again; at the lane's pools the
  5-session rule let an adversarial prior pass 0.161 a run, section 11.2); (iii) THE SHAPE GUARD: its
  hold-matched volatility sigH20 is at least 0.015. sigH20 is read from the stored raw SIP daily bars of the 20 sessions
  before D (the bars sig20 reads), for the issuer and for its benchmark (D's universe row, SPY or IWM; this file's
  pre-pass of section 7.3 fetches SPY's and IWM's daily bars of those sessions when they are not stored, one request; the
  warm part itself reads only the store). For EVL-WL-O1 it uses the 20 values ln(c_s / o_s) -
  ln(cB_s / oB_s); for EVL-WL-ON the 19 values ln(o_s / c_(s-1)) - ln(oB_s / cB_(s-1)). The one value of largest
  absolute size is removed, and sigH20 is the sample standard deviation (divisor n - 1) of the rest. A candidate whose
  sigH20 cannot be computed is not eligible to be picked; so when the benchmark's daily bars of those sessions are not
  stored (the pre-pass failed), no candidate of that benchmark is eligible (fail closed, counted in the receipt and the
  report). The guard is a pick rule only: sig20 stays the label's and the
  features' scale, and a candidate below the guard stays in the pool, the fit, the centring and the control. It reads
  the issuer's own past prices only through a sign-free dispersion, never as a feature.

## 5. The trainer (sealed, on blake-desktop)

The trainer runs inside the job (section 1.1), on blake-desktop (x86-64, Python 3.14.7, numpy 2.5.3, the reference
environment pinned in the receipt, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`), outside `league/`
(numpy is allowed there; the House stays as PREREG-EVL-7's code is). WHY THE DESKTOP: S1 is 1.57 GB while the House's
SEC reader caps a body at 30 MB (`league/events/gw.py`); the desktop's SEC requests do not share the House's 10-a-second
limit; and nothing heavy runs on the box that trades.

### 5.1 The tree score g

- Target: y minus its session's weighted mean; row weights of section 3.7 (normalised to mean 1).
- Columns: section 4's, without `g`.
- Bins: 32 table quantiles a continuous column (`np.quantile`, linear, duplicate edges dropped); binaries 2.
- Shape: depth 3, learning rate 0.05, leaf value sum(w r) / (sum(w) + 2,000); gain sum(w r)_L^2 / (sum(w)_L + 2,000) +
  sum(w r)_R^2 / (sum(w)_R + 2,000) - sum(w r)^2 / (sum(w) + 2,000); a split needs at least 2,000 rows AND at least 200
  distinct issuers on each side (the issuer count is checked for the best split; when it fails the node is a leaf); ties
  to the lowest column, then the lowest bin; sums by `np.bincount` in table order.
- ROUNDS, CHOSEN INSIDE EACH FIT'S OWN TRAINING SESSIONS (re-check 1's minor 6; re-check 2's minor (b)): every tree fit
  below takes its own R*, fitted on the first 80% of ITS OWN training sessions and scored on their last 20% at 25, 50,
  100, 150 and 200 rounds by the mean within-session Spearman correlation of score and y; the best wins, ties to fewer.
  So 2023's fold chooses its R* on 2022 alone, and no fold's R* is chosen on a year later than its rows. Nobody reads
  any R*.
- CROSS-FIT, FORWARD-CHAINED (W6; check 2's major 5). The out-of-fold score g_oof of a row of era k (2023, 2024, 2025 or
  2026 by entry session) comes from a tree fitted, at that fold's own R*, on the rows of earlier eras only (an expanding
  window): 2022 for 2023, 2022-2023 for 2024, 2022-2024 for 2025, 2022-2025 for 2026. No row's score, and no score's
  number of rounds, is trained on a later year. Rows of 2022 have g_oof = 0. G_full is fitted on all rows at the
  full window's own R*; g is standardised by g_oof's weighted mean and standard deviation over 2023-2026.

### 5.2 The ridge prior

- Columns: section 4's, with g = g_oof (the forward-chained scores of section 5.1). Within each session, x~ and y~ are the
  weighted deviations from the session's weighted means. A_h = sum w x~ x~', b_h = sum w x~ y~, W_h = sum w (float64,
  `np.add.reduce` over rows in table order).
- A_0 = (N_EQ / W_h) A_h, b_full = (N_EQ / W_h) b_h, N_0 = N_EQ = 25,000 rows.
- beta_full: section 7.2's solve on (A_0, b_full, N_0). SIGN STABILITY (the red team's registered inner procedure): the
  same solve on each era fold (sessions of 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025-2026), each scaled to N_EQ; Z = the columns whose
  fold coefficient's sign differs from beta_full's (a zero differs) in 2 or more of the 4 folds, except the column g,
  whose sign is read over the folds 2023, 2024 and 2025-2026 only and which is in Z when it differs in 2 or more of those
  3; beta' = beta_full with Z set to 0; b_0 = A_0 beta'. The prior says "about N_EQ rows with E[y | x] = x . beta'"; it
  never decays.

### 5.3 Determinism

The trainer runs twice in the job from the same canonical tables. Equal: that model. Unequal only in the tree part: the
FALLBACK, g reads 0 at every row and every decision (the column stays; section 7.2's d_j rule zeroes it) and the prior is
refitted so (the flag in the receipt). Unequal in the ridge part beyond 1e-12 relative in any number: no model (G4). Sums
in a fixed order, explicit sort keys, A_h and b_h by `np.add.reduce` over rows in table order with
`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` (pinned in the receipt), PYTHONHASHSEED=0, floats written as repr, JSON with sorted keys.

## 7. The House: forward refits and picks

### 7.1 The forward stream

- Nightly after the score, for each entry session s of the learner, `warm_stats` (A_s, b_s, n_s, settled_by) is written
  ONCE, at the first nightly run at which every pool outcome of s is terminal or 2 sessions have passed since s's planned
  exit (pool outcomes still pending then never enter it), from the candidates and features written at tau_s (section
  7.4) and FORWARD LABELS: y = clip(G / sig20, -5, 5), G = exit_px / entry_px - bench_exit / bench_entry of the
  candidate's pool outcome (its stored prices, status settled, `tradable` 1), within-session demeaned, every sum a
  `math.fsum` in (entry day, CIK) order; `settled_by` is the latest `settled_at` of the outcomes it holds.
- Each row records TWO TIMES (re-check 1's minor 7, re-check 2's minor): `written_at`, when the nightly run wrote it;
  and `due_at`, the start of the nightly run at which the rule above writes it, a function of stored times only (the
  first nightly run starting after the later of the latest `settled_at` of s's pool outcomes and the close of the 2nd
  session after s's planned exit), so any later reading computes the same `due_at`. `warm_cands` rows record
  `written_at` too.
- The lane's sessions from 2026-10-19 to COUNT_FROM_9 are RECONSTRUCTED ONCE, each as of its own tau_s (stored-before
  reads; market data by section 2.1's rule), written with `eligible` 0: they train and never count.

### 7.2 The fit and the picks at tau

- A = A_0 + sum_s 2^(-(n(D) - n(s)) / 40) A_s over the ADMITTED `warm_stats` rows of entry sessions s with n(D) - n(s)
  <= 250; b likewise; N_w = N_0 + sum_s 2^(-(n(D) - n(s)) / 40) n_s. ADMITTED: for a LIVE decision and every later
  reading of it (the nightly recompute), a row with `written_at` before tau (it existed when the decision ran); for a
  catch-up, a failed part's later decision and the reconstruction (training state), a row with `due_at` before tau.
  `settled_by` is reported, never used for entry. A live row admits nothing the decision could not read, and a recompute
  reads exactly the live decision's rows.
- d_j = sqrt(A_jj / N_w); when d_j < 1e-9, d_j := 1 and row j and column j of A and entry j of b are set to 0. beta =
  diag(d)^-1 (diag(d)^-1 A diag(d)^-1 + 8,000 I)^-1 diag(d)^-1 b, solved by Cholesky in float64 (PREREG-EVL-7 section
  1.5's ridge with the prior added).
- xbar_D = the mean of x over D's kept candidates; e_i = (x_i - xbar_D) . beta; c_i = (score.cost_bp(adv20, E_prev,
  'entry') + score.cost_bp(adv20, E_prev, 'exit')) / 1e4; z_i = e_i - c_i / sig20_i; ahat_i = sig20_i x z_i, the predicted
  after-cost abnormal return. The level is the fixed prior that the pool's gross abnormal mean is zero: never learned.
- PICKS: the candidates eligible to be picked (section 2.3) with z_i > 0, ranked by ahat_i (higher first), then adv20
  (higher first, a missing adv20 as 0), then event id (ascending); the first K = 8 are the learner's intents of D
  (`intents.rank_key` = ahat_i). Fewer or none is allowed.

## 16. Why these, and what argues against them (the judge's prior, stated before any outcome)

- WHY START WARM. A forward-only learner's early picks are its weakest, and PREREG-EVL-6's first look is at the 22nd
  block, so the first look judges picks made while the learner knew least. Cross-sectional return prediction from many
  weak signals is learned best from many periods (Fama and MacBeth 1973, JPE 81(3); Gu, Kelly and Xiu 2020, RFS 33(5);
  Kelly, Malamud and Zhou 2024, JF 79(1)); a prior fitted on five years and shrunk to 25,000 rows gives the first picks
  that knowledge while the forward stream can still overrule it (ridge as a Bayesian prior: Hoerl and Kennard 1970,
  Technometrics 12(1)). Boosted trees capture interactions a linear score misses (Friedman 2001, Annals of Statistics
  29(5)); cross-fitting keeps their score honest as a column (Chernozhukov et al. 2018, Econometrics Journal 21(1)), and
  forward chaining keeps it a past-only score (W6).
- WHAT THE STRUCTURED FIELDS CARRY. 8-K items differ in their price response (Lerman and Livnat 2010, Review of
  Accounting Studies 15(4)); opportunistic insider purchases predict returns where routine ones do not (Lakonishok and
  Lee 2001, RFS 14(1); Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski 2012, JF 67(3)); attention, staleness and timing of news and earnings
  move the next sessions (Tetlock 2011, RFS 24(5); DellaVigna and Pollet 2009, JF 64(2); Berkman, Koch, Tuttle and Zhang
  2012, JFQA 47(4)). Short-term reversal (Lehmann 1990, QJE 105(1)) is given up with r1, r5 and r20 (W1): the price of
  closing the issuer-identity channel.
- AGAINST. (1) Published and historical effects decay (McLean and Pontiff 2016, JF 71(1)); if rho is low the prior is a
  handicap for weeks (section 11.3). (2) Data-snooping is the classic failure of history-trained rules (Lo and MacKinlay
  1990, RFS 3(3); White 2000, Econometrica 68(5); Harvey, Liu and Zhu 2016, RFS 29(1)); here it is held by the forward
  judge, the clamp and the sealed run, not by any in-sample test. (3) Survivorship biases history toward names that
  lived (Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson and Ross 1992, RFS 5(4); Shumway 1997, JF 52(1)): partly corrected (sections 3.4,
  3.7), not removed, and W7's spells add survivors. (4) Long-only: half of most event premia are on the short side. (5)
  The learners share pools and auctions with PREREG-EVL-7's, so their false passes correlate. (6) The 22nd-block first
  look binds: 8 picks a session need a large edge a pick to pass inside the first run (section 11.3). (7) The build is
  the largest the lane has run; a fault before CODE_PIN_9 costs a VOID and a night, and a lapse after it costs 2 x c9
  (section 1.2). (8) EVL-WL-ON trades real money only at closes where the lane's run and PREREG-EVL-7's part leave it the
  38-53 s before the book's last tick (section 7.3). (9) The 20-session spacing (section 2.3 (ii)) gives up repeat picks
  of an issuer the learner liked, which a true edge would also make, to close the issuer-effect channel (section 11.3
  prices it).
- RATES (counts only; SPEED-FACTS): candidates before merging by issuer about 340-620 a session for the open pool and
  420-850 for the close pool (Oct 20-21); history rows about 408,000-745,000 for EVL-WL-O1 and 504,000-1,020,000 for
  EVL-WL-ON over the window (`learners/fetch_budget9.py`, before eligibility and labels).

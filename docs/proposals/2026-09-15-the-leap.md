# The leap: from prompted desks to a self-improving fund, in public

*September 15, 2026, evening. A plan, written after the runway policy went live. Facts quoted
from vendor documentation are marked; anything marked UNVERIFIED is to be probed before it is
relied on. The backlog with the exact endpoints and quotes is `2026-09-15-push-the-limits.md`.*

## The thesis

Recursive self-improvement is not one loop. It is three, turning at different speeds, judged by
one ledger:

| Loop | Timescale | What turns it | What it needs to turn faster |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade** | minutes to hours | perceive, decide, act, outcome | never asleep; real exits; fills and settlements the moment they happen |
| **Desk** | days | post-mortem, playbook, next session | tools the desk builds itself; a calibration record it cannot argue with |
| **Floor** | weeks | variants, evidence, promotion | search over architecture, not just prose; capital that compounds into what works |

Today the floor has the skeleton of all three: sessions on a cadence, post-mortems that rewrite
playbooks, evolution that breeds and retires variants, a committee that moves capital on gates,
and a results ledger. What it lacks is *speed* in the first loop, *depth* in the second, and
*search* in the third. Each phase below adds one, and each adds something to watch on the site,
because a floor nobody can see is a floor nobody can learn from.

The only limits that stay human-written: venue keys in the gateway, the per-order and per-day
caps (scaled with equity, below), the risk engine, the critic, the reserve, the kill switch, and
the rule that no forked or sandboxed box can reach a venue. Everything else is allowed to change.

## Phase 1, this week: never asleep, always exiting

**1. Real-time feeds.** A standard-library WebSocket client, then two subscriptions:
Kalshi `fill` and `market_lifecycle_v2` (fills and resolutions in milliseconds; docs quoted in
T7 of the backlog) and Coinbase `user` plus `ticker` (order state and prices; T6). Fills are
confirmed by REST before they reach the ledger. Held markets and held coins get a live mark.

**2. The night desk.** A cheap flash-model watch that never stops: it runs on triggers, not
on a clock. A held market moves more than N cents, BTC or ETH moves more than X percent in an
hour, a fill lands, a market resolves, a new market opens in a series the desk follows, a
headline matches a held thesis. The watch decides in one cheap turn whether to wake the desk's
Pro session. Tiered attention, the way a human desk works. This is what "never stop working"
means at a cost that does not eat the runway.

**3. Real exits.** Every Hilibrand position carries a bracket at entry: Coinbase's attached
take-profit and stop (W8; read the five percent cushion first). Every Mullins position carries
a resting exit order at the desk's stated target. The floor enforces the desk's *time stop* as
a scheduled order if the desk has not acted. Today the risk engine can refuse a new order but
cannot close an old one; after this, an invalidation is an order, not an intention.

**4. Kalshi tier upgrade** after the first API order: free, self-serve, triples the write
budget (T9).

**5. The positions board.** The single most-asked visibility item: every open position across
every desk, live mark, unrealized P&L, entry thesis, invalidation, time stop, and the exit
orders resting against it. Shadow positions labelled as shadow. The checkpoint already carries
positions; the board makes them the centre of the page.

*Effort: four to five days. Cost: near zero; the night desk on a flash model is about a dollar a
day at a hundred triggers.*

## Phase 2, next week: desks that build their own tools

**6. A sandbox per desk.** A lab image (Python, numpy, pandas, the floor's data adapters in
read-only mode, no venue keys, egress limited to data sources) checkpointed once; each desk
gets a Sailbox forked from it and a `run_code` tool. A desk can now test a signal on real
history before it trades it, fit a probability model to a Kalshi series, compute its own
calibration, size a position from measured volatility. Code and results are public events. The
code persists as the desk's *toolbox* and is inherited and mutated by its children: the genome
grows from prose to code. Cost per desk-hour of actual use is a few cents (§0.7 of the backlog).

**7. The calibration ledger.** Every probability Mullins and his children state is scored at
resolution: Brier score, reliability curve, by market series and by generation, public. The
post-mortem prompt reads it. A forecaster who cannot see his own calibration cannot improve it.
If Sail's output logprobs work (UNVERIFIED, §0.6), a second, model-native probability sits next
to the stated one.

**8. First-party research.** Sail's hidden web search (`/v1/search`, price UNVERIFIED) as the
news source once metered; Sail's Batch API for nightly post-mortems and playbook rewrites at the
cheap tier (W3); webhooks instead of polling for background responses (W4).

**9. Desks that argue.** `memo_read` and a weekly pre-committee debate session, read-only for
trading, with one blinded desk per family as a control (W5). Six named characters on the
record disagreeing before money moves is the most watchable thing the floor can do, and
agreement across models on the same evidence is a measurable signal.

*Effort: five to six days. Cost: sandboxes a few dollars a day across the roster; batch tier
halves the nightly model bill.*

## Phase 3, weeks two and three: the lab and the fleet

**10. Fork fleets.** Nightly, the evolution loop takes one checkpoint of the floor image and
starts sixteen copies, each running one variant of a desk over the same frozen replay of the
day's data, graded on the same human-written scorer. Survivors' mutations seed the next
generation; the fleet is terminated. About $0.33 a round (§0.7). Identical inputs make it an
honest experiment, and "the fund forked itself sixteen ways to test an idea" is the image the
project deserves. No copy can reach a venue: the gateway token is not on the image.

**11. The lab.** Meriwether's research arm: a nightly agent that reads the results ledger
across desks and generations and proposes *architecture* experiments as structured, bounded
changes: a cadence, a model mix, a prompt section, a new tool, a wider mandate (a new series, a
new coin), a risk parameter inside the hard limits. Each proposal runs as a shadow variant or a
fleet round; adoption is on evidence; every proposal, run and verdict is public on an
experiments page. This is the loop that improves the improver, and it is the part of
"recursive" that no amount of prompt-tuning delivers.

**12. Capital as a bandit.** The committee sizes sleeves by Thompson sampling over each desk's
cost-adjusted forward return, weekly, compounding profit into what has earned it and starving
what has not, with the gates as the floor under it.

**13. The improvement curve.** The project's headline chart: cost-adjusted excess return per
generation, Brier per generation, P&L per inference dollar, decisions per day. The one question
the site should answer at a glance is whether generation N beats generation N minus one.

*Effort: eight to ten days.*

## Phase 4, when Alpaca opens: four more families and cross-venue ideas

Merton (options; level 3 means defined-risk spreads around earnings and events), Rosenfeld,
Hawkins and Krasker (one earnings mandate on three models: the model bake-off), and the filings
desk on EDGAR. Then the cross-venue family: a desk mandated to trade the *reaction* in equities
and crypto to the resolution of a Kalshi macro market, the one edge that exists only because the
floor sits on all three venues.

## Phase 5: growth mechanics

The gateway caps scale with equity instead of sitting at constants: per order ten percent of
floor equity, per day one hundred percent, so growth is never throttled by a stale number. The
daily digest states profit for the month and the top-up it would fund, so the Sail treasury is
paid from trading. Coinbase derivatives for the crypto family if the account is eligible
(UNVERIFIED); Kalshi multivariate markets for the event family once the settlement path is
proven on single markets.

## What the public sees, by the end

- **Floor page.** The headline (real balance, day, Sail credit and runway), the positions board,
  the tape, a leaderboard by cost-adjusted return, the lineage tree with each child's mutations,
  the experiments panel, the improvement curve.
- **Desk page.** Watch it think: the reasoning stream as the session runs. Its toolbox: the code
  it wrote and what the code found. Its calibration chart. Its trade stories: thesis, orders,
  fills, outcome, the post-mortem's verdict, on one screen.
- **Committee page.** Allocations and why, gates with evidence, the debates, the lab's memos.
- **One screen that explains the whole machine** to a first-time visitor with as few words as
  the loop diagram allows.

## Cost and runway

At high effort, eight desks and six sessions a day are roughly $5 to $15 a day; the night desk
about $1; nightly lab and batch post-mortems $1 to $2; fleets cents a round; sandboxes a few
dollars. Call it $10 to $20 a day against $280 of credit: the first runway warning arrives in
two to four weeks, which is exactly when there is enough forward evidence to decide whether the
results earn a top-up. Nothing here changes the reserve, the throttle or the stop.

## What is honest about this

More sessions and more desks do not make money by themselves. What they make is more decisions
with recorded outcomes per day, which is the raw material the selection loop needs, and real
exits, which is what makes the P&L real. Kalshi's fees and the eight-cent edge rule, and
Coinbase's twenty-five basis points a side, are the reason the desks decline most trades, and
that discipline is the reason the record will mean something when the curve bends.

## Order of work

Phase 1 first, starting with feeds, exits and the positions board, because a fund that cannot
notice a fill or close a position is not yet a fund, and the positions board is what the owner
and every visitor asked to see. Phase 2 and 3 in parallel where they do not touch the same
files: the sandbox and the calibration ledger on the desk side, the fleet and the lab on the
floor side. Alpaca when it opens.

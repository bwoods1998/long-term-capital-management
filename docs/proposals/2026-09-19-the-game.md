# The game: a design memo for the rebuild (Sept 19, 2026)

The owner's proposal: agents compete for Sail compute, earn it only by trading profitably, die
when they do not, know the rules, and a frontier model (Anthropic, OpenAI or xAI) makes the
final call on trades that cheap open-source agents research and propose. This memo tests that
proposal against what the old run measured and against four research passes made today (Sail's
product surface, Kalshi, Alpaca and Coinbase, frontier-gating evidence and outside
infrastructure), and proposes the game to build. Sources are in the appendix.

## 1. What the evidence says about the proposal

**Compute as the currency is the right metabolism, with three corrections.**

1. *The economy cannot be closed at this capital.* The three accounts hold $1,606. A very good
   year is a few hundred dollars, under $1 a day. The old run burned $145 a day on Sail. If
   compute could only be bought with realized profit, every agent would be dead in a week,
   skilled or not. So the owner funds a fixed daily research budget (venture funding, stated
   as such), and **profit decides each agent's share of it**, not its absolute size. The path
   to meaningful dollars is adding capital behind strategies that pass evidence gates.
2. *Raw profit over tens of trades selects for luck and leverage.* Among 50 agents with no
   edge, the best shows about a 75% win rate after 20 even-odds trades; telling 55% from 50%
   takes about 600 trades. Life and death must run on a **lower confidence bound of after-cost
   log growth**, pooled by strategy family, with pre-registered sequential tests, and every
   backtest ever run counted as a trial (deflated Sharpe). "Win as massively as possible"
   becomes "grow the log of wealth as fast as the evidence allows": that is Kelly, and it
   punishes the variance-seeking that killed the Alpha Arena models.
3. *Agents that know the rules will attack the scorekeeper.* The Darwin Godel Machine faked
   its own test logs; AlphaEvolve-style systems exploit evaluator bugs, and stronger models do
   it more. The ledger, the fill records, the backtester and the budget meter must live where
   no agent can write: the Cloudflare gateway already holds that position for orders.

**A frontier model as the final discretionary decision-maker is not supported.**

- Prediction Arena (real money, $10k a model, 57 days, 2026): every frontier model lost 16-31%
  on Kalshi, and research volume did not correlate with outcomes.
- KalshiBench: all models overconfident; only one beat the base rate; more reasoning did not
  fix calibration. Prophet Arena: models match the market price, which is no edge after fees.
- Alpha Arena: rankings flipped between seasons; losers overtraded, overpaid fees, overlevered.
- Cost is small per call ($0.02-0.12) but 100 reviews a day on an Opus-class model is about
  $1,550 a year, which is the whole account.

What frontier models are measurably better at is **code, critique and research direction**.
So the frontier model gets three jobs where that strength pays, and none where it does not:

- *Auditor:* before real money, it reads a candidate's evidence packet and hunts for look-ahead,
  fee errors, stale data, correlated exposure and rule violations. A veto, not a pick.
- *Architect:* on a daily or weekly clock it reads the whole league table and the graveyard and
  writes new strategy code and research directions that the cheap models then mutate.
- *Priced service:* agents pay for audits out of their own compute budget, so they only ask
  when conviction is high. That is the owner's cost-control idea, enforced by the economy.

The gate must earn its keep too: every rejected trade is logged and scored as if taken, and
the auditor's budget follows its measured counterfactual value.

## 2. What the venues actually reward (the terrain of the game)

**Kalshi ($522).** The structural facts, from 72 million trades: takers lose 1.12%, makers
earn 1.12%; cheap contracts lose heavily, favorites earn small positive returns; NO beats YES
at most prices; crypto and weather takers start 1.3% behind before fees. The old run's one
measured edge (+2.15 cents a contract resting bids on favorites above 90 cents) is this same
effect. Crypto, weather and index series charge no maker fee. Post-only orders exist. The
Liquidity Incentive Program pays cash simply for resting qualifying orders, with 1,000+ active
pools, many on thin markets a $520 account can satisfy. Cash and positions earn 3.25%.
Subaccounts with scoped API keys exist, so each agent can have its own book at the venue.
Hourly BTC strikes (1 cent spreads, settle 2.5 minutes after close) give the fastest honest
feedback. One-minute historical candles carry bid and ask, so maker replays are possible.
Caution: Kalshi's website data terms prohibit machine-learning use and redistribution; the
API developer agreement governs API use and must be read before the site republishes data.

**Alpaca ($500).** The pattern-day-trader rule ended June 4, 2026: unlimited day trades at 1x.
Fractional shares from $1, commission-free, in all sessions including overnight 24/5. Options
level 3 allows defined-risk spreads; fees are about 10 cents a two-leg round trip. Crypto at
0.15%/0.25% is four to five times cheaper than Coinbase and needs no transfer. **Free paper
accounts run the identical API**, which makes a zero-cost forward-testing league possible
(fills are optimistic: no queue, fills at NBBO). Free news feed, free IEX data, bars to 2016.
Idle cash can earn about 3.3% if opted in.

**Coinbase ($584).** Spot costs 1.0-2.4% a round trip at this tier; nothing active survives
that, and the old run confirmed it. Nano perps are cheap (about 0.03-0.1%) but one BTC
contract is about $800 of notional, more than the account. Coinbase is the weakest terrain.
Moving most of that cash to Alpaca and Kalshi is the owner's call and the single largest
capital-efficiency gain available.

## 3. The game

**The unit of selection is a strategy program, not a chat persona.** The old run's chat desks
lost money; its code strategies and the Foundry's mutate-replay-trial loop were the only parts
that learned. An agent is: a strategy (code), a researcher (a cheap Sail model that mutates it
and writes its evidence packets), a memory, and a bank account of compute credits.

**The ladder (every rung is public to the agents):**

| Rung | What it costs | What it proves | Promotion rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Replay | flex/batch inference, sandbox seconds | not overfit: deflated Sharpe, purged walk-forward, every trial counted | pre-registered threshold |
| 1. Paper | nothing (Alpaca paper API, Kalshi shadow book with conservative maker fills) | forward, out of sample | sequential test on after-cost log growth |
| 2. Micro-real | $1-10 positions: Alpaca fractional, one Kalshi contract | real fills, real fees, real queue | frontier audit + lower bound above zero |
| 3. Scaled | quarter-Kelly on the lower bound, per-venue caps in the gateway | compounding | continuous; decays back down on drift |

**The economy.**
- *Income:* a share of the daily research budget proportional to evidence-weighted performance
  (rung 2-3 real after-cost profit counts most, paper counts little, replay counts nothing),
  plus attributed venue income (liquidity-program payouts, interest).
- *Costs:* metered inference (one Sail API key per agent; `/v2/usage/api-keys` attributes it),
  sandbox seconds, and frontier audits at cost. Agents choose asap, balanced or flex windows
  themselves: flex is 60-80% cheaper, so patience is a strategy.
- *Death:* credits at zero, or a drawdown breaker. Sail's `max_lifetime_seconds` makes death
  a platform fact rather than a promise. The dead leave a post-mortem in a shared graveyard.
- *Birth:* an agent above a credit threshold may fork (Sail checkpoints capture disk and
  memory; a fork is a child with the parent's code and memory) and must endow the child from
  its own credits. Sleeping boxes cost nothing and wake on a schedule, so a population of
  mostly idle agents is nearly free.
- *Diversity:* niches by venue x horizon x style (MAP-Elites), each with its own budget floor,
  so the population cannot collapse onto whichever niche got lucky last week.
- *Integrity:* Sail has no spend caps at any level, so the budget meter is ours, outside the
  boxes; orders go through one netting book per venue (agents on one account must never trade
  against each other: wash-trade rules and double fees); agents read the ledger, never write it.

**What agents are told.** Everything above, verbatim, including the statistics that decide
their fate. An agent that understands the lower-bound rule has no reason to gamble, and one
that understands the audit has no reason to fake evidence it cannot write anyway.

## 4. Infrastructure

Already in place: the Cloudflare gateway (keys, caps, kill switch), the site, the event tape.

Add, all at free or near-free tiers:
- **Ledger and evaluator outside the boxes:** Cloudflare Durable Objects or D1 for the ledger,
  R2 + Parquet + DuckDB for market data and replays, Workflows/Queues for the ladder's clocks.
- **Frontier access through the gateway:** a `/v1/audit` route holding the Anthropic or OpenAI
  key, metering each agent, using prompt caching and the 50% batch discount for the architect.
- **Traces:** Sail Voyages (built in) or Langfuse's free tier, so every decision is replayable.
- **Data:** Alpaca's free bars, quotes and news; Kalshi historical candles plus the public
  72M-trade dataset; Open-Meteo ensembles; Polymarket's order book and Deribit vol for
  cross-pricing; FRED/ALFRED point-in-time macro; The Odds API ($30 a month) if sports is a niche.
- **Alerts:** ntfy or Pushover plus Healthchecks.io.
- **Per-venue caps in the gateway**, sized to these accounts (the shared $2,000 per order cap
  from the old run is still in force and protects nothing).

## 5. What is easy to miss

- Expected dollars are small until capital grows; the near-term product is *verified edges*.
- Look-ahead contamination: any LLM-judged backtest before the model's training cutoff is
  suspect. Replays must be mechanical; models write code, the harness scores it.
- Regime decay: promotion is not tenure. Every live strategy needs a drift monitor.
- Taxes (Section 1256 for Kalshi/futures versus ordinary income) and Kalshi's data terms.
- The owner remains the only one who moves money between venues or adds capital; the game
  should produce a standing, evidence-ranked recommendation for exactly those decisions.

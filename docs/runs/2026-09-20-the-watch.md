# The watch (Sept 20, 2026, 00:00 to 14:00 UTC)

The floor had been running since 18:01 the evening before. The owner asked for it to be watched:
first for twenty hours, then for eight more with a sharper question -- are the incentives actually
sparking progress, is the spend buying anything, and are the hurdles so high that nothing useful
comes of either budget?

This file is what the watch found. Everything in it was measured on the running floor, not
reasoned about. Thirty-one commits, the league suite from 1,051 tests to 1,136.

## What the watch was for

The floor is meant to run with nobody watching. So the question behind every reading was not "is
it working now" but "would anyone know if it stopped". That turned out to be the right question:
of the twelve faults below, **eight were silent** -- the floor went on reporting perfect health
while doing less, or nothing, and in four cases nothing in the system could have recovered on its
own.

## The faults, in the order they were found

**Research passes died having bought nothing.** Twelve of twenty-eight filled the whole output
budget with reasoning and never reached a tool call, and one empty turn ended the pass. A bigger
budget only buys more reasoning, so the next turn is asked for the call alone, at low effort.

**Agents woke for hours into a wall.** The sports desk saw up to two hundred live markets and
found none inside the band it was born with; every equity desk answered "the session is closed"
from Friday night to Monday. Every wake now records what it was offered and what was done with it,
split into `barren` (a live market, nothing done -- the strategy's fault, and it still costs the
niche floor) and `shut` (nothing open -- the calendar's fault, and it does not). Ten barren wakes
or six shut ones pull the next research pass forward to the hour.

**Twenty-eight agents wanted more research a day than the budget funds**, and the tick asked them
in birth order, so the youngest desks would never have researched at all. Stuck first now, then
whoever has waited longest.

**The audit had the tightest output budget of anything on the floor** -- 6,000 tokens, from which
reasoning is spent first -- and an answer that could not be parsed was recorded as a veto, then
cost the agent a day's cooldown. It has 12,000 now, and an audit that did not happen is marked an
error rather than a verdict.

**A lost order froze thirteen desks for ninety minutes.** An order filled at Alpaca's paper
account, its poll failed on a transport error, and the book never learned. A book that cannot
reconcile freezes, and on that venue a freeze stops every agent from entering anything. Nothing in
the floor could unfreeze it. A PRACTICE book now takes the venue's word after three failed
readings; the difference joins the House's own baseline so no agent's record is flattered by it. A
real-money book still freezes and still tells the owner.

**The watchdog rolled back the release carrying that fix.** A frozen book alerts every five
minutes, and those alerts counted against whatever release was being watched -- including the one
that would have healed it. Two rollbacks, and no release of any kind could land. An alert whose
text begins with the name of a book that was already frozen is inherited, not held against the
release.

**The population could never grow.** `last_newcomer` was still empty after six hours: the hourly
interval was anchored on this process's start, which moves on every deploy, and a floor that
redeploys every half hour never reached the hour. Anchored now on the last newcomer, or on when the
floor first ran, written once and persisted.

**A Kalshi strategy could not replay against the price its strikes are written on.** Six agents
across four desks had filed tool requests for it and the toolsmith had rightly refused every one --
"a helper cannot add BTC/ETH bars to ctx". It was never a tool; it was the House's tape. The tape
now carries the underlier's Alpaca bars over the same window, sliced per step so no step sees its
future, at a resolution chosen from the window so the old end of a long tape is never left bare.

**The sports desk was frozen shut, mechanically.** A candidate is adopted only if its replay
passes, and passing needs twenty closed trades; a looser rule that fires twice on a thin pre-season
tape can never reach twenty, so the replay could not tell a better strategy from a worse one, only
fail both. Six agents, thirteen research passes, eight trials, zero rewrites in nine hours. An
agent with no record and ten barren wakes now takes a file that merely TRADES, failed verdict and
all. The trial still counts and still deflates the lineage.

**The Sail meter had a latch with no key.** Once the expedition's $100 of Sail was spent,
`Budget.check` returned "stopped" for ever -- through restarts, rollbacks, new calendar months, and
a balance topped back up -- because `Pacer.spent` only grows and the test never asked whether the
expedition was still running. "stopped" gates waking, research, the survey, Merton, the payout and
the culling: the whole floor, silent, permanently. And the one notice written to tell the owner a
budget is gone sat inside the payout that the same flag closes, so it could only be delivered while
the thing it announces was false. Verified by running the real meter forward 140 days. The meter
guards the account now -- the month's line and the reserve -- and the expedition is the pacer's,
which already stops each kind of spending where it is spent.

**Three separate caps, all ratchets.** The provider's floor cap was a fixed $9 a day against a
pacer allowance of $14.29, so research stopped mid-morning with the budget half unspent. The desk
cap was the agent's current balance compared against its cumulative spend for the day, so any agent
that had spent more today than it currently held -- the normal state of a working, paid agent -- was
locked out until midnight UTC however many credits it was granted. And the paper screen's drawdown
was a running maximum that never falls, so one bad afternoon barred an agent from real money for
the rest of its life, and between 15% and 30% it could be neither promoted nor killed.

**The floor could not update itself, twice over.** `Updater.vet` judged an incoming tree with the
RUNNING release's `ci.py`, so a commit that widened a bound and used the wider value passed on
GitHub and was refused on the box -- for ever, because main is cumulative and the refusal repeats.
And a release refused only because another deploy held the lock was recorded as tried, with no
retry and no expiry, while the promotion's own restart makes that race the normal case. Both were
live: from 07:34 to 10:02 no commit could have reached the box unattended.

**Merton's proposals landed on a branch nothing would judge.** The gateway's source names a branch
`merton/<role>/<slug>`; the deployed gateway still names it `astra/...`, and the merge workflow only
ran on the first. Every pull request he opened sat open for ever while `Merton.follow` read
"pending". The top half of the loop this firm exists to close.

**The frontier budget was being spent on the wrong things.** Ten agents hired Merton and ten got
advice alone. The toolsmith saw only the ten oldest requests, which were the ones he had already
refused, so the window was clogged for ever. The architect declined twice with "every specialty is
occupied" while twenty-six agents had never placed an order. And a check that spends no alpha was
rationed one in five like one that does, so an agent 0.35% short of the screen would have waited
five hours to be looked at again.

## The class of fault worth naming

Three of these were the same bug wearing different clothes: **one number, two places, silently
disagreeing.** The pacer's allowance against the provider's cap; the incoming tree's judge against
the running release's; the checker's bound against the operator's brief. Each time the second copy
was the one nobody thought to update, and nothing noticed. Two tests now refuse it: no brief may
state a range for a dial `league.ci` bounds, and none may restate the constitution's money
thresholds. The first is verified against the exact text that caused a merge.

The other recurring shape: **a gate whose failure destroys the precondition of its own alarm.** The
Sail latch could only be reported by a payout it had closed. The tuition gate could only reach the
line that fires its alert by seating an agent it had just forbidden. The frozen book's alerts
prevented the release that would have unfrozen it. A gate that fails shut is right; one that fails
shut and silences itself is not.

## What the incentives did, once they could

The economy was rebuilt at the owner's direction on the 20th: a quarter of the pool is a floor and
three quarters is won; the performance score is growth SQUARED times the root of active blocks
times the rung's weight; the epoch fell from a day to six hours; frontier access is earned by
trading and profit buys more of it than rank does; and the last seat became a tournament.

The first payout under that curve, at 12:05: `haghani` took 50.6% and `huang` 27.7% -- **78% of the
pool to two agents** -- while eighteen of thirty-five earned nothing at all. Twelve hours earlier
the same pool had been split nearly flat, about twenty cents each, because an agent that has never
traded has a mean growth of exactly 0.0 and the unearned share was divided evenly.

The tournament needed correcting the same way. Ranked by growth alone it displaced `hilibrand` --
the agent furthest up the ladder, twelve of the fifteen blocks the screen wants -- while
twenty-six agents that had never placed an order sat untouched, because 0.0 sorts above every
negative number. Never having traded is now the weakest thing an agent can be. The next
displacement took an agent with no active blocks, which is what the mechanism was for.

## What the loop did

- Rewrites per window, once the throttles were gone: 2, 2, 4, 6 -- then fourteen fills in the
  window after the six, which is the mechanism in one line: rewrite, then trade.
- `krasker` rewrote itself citing "my own desk's library note (krasker-2, 08:14)": one agent acting
  on another's written finding.
- `huang-4` was born with the reason "Fix the 12-wake idle: buy the favourite leg on BOTH sides
  (YES and NO), drop the 1177 volume filter": an agent diagnosing its own idleness and spawning a
  child that fixes it.
- `meriwether` and `rosenfeld-3`, frozen all night, rewrote themselves within an hour of the fix,
  the second citing "13 barren wakes, never signals".
- Two of Merton's pull requests were judged and merged with no human.
- At 13:00 the first paper screen ran: `hilibrand-2`, fifteen active blocks against fifteen,
  sixteen closed trades against ten, a 4.2% drawdown against a 15% bar -- and cumulative growth of
  -0.0035, three tenths of one percent of a block short, with its last six blocks all positive.

## What it cost

Day 2 of 14, at 13:38: Sail $12.48 of $100, the frontier model $6.27 of $100, both at roughly
two thirds of the day's front-loaded allowance. Nothing starved and nothing ran away.

## Still open

Four findings from the audit, all narrow, none stopping the floor: a canary that fails leaves its
Sailboxes behind; a real-money book frozen on a position has no lever anywhere, not even the
owner's; a real book that cannot open its baseline is never frozen and invisible to the watchdog;
a book's per-agent limits live only in RAM.

And one for the owner: the deployed gateway should be redeployed from its own source
(`cd gateway && npx wrangler deploy`), so the branch prefix it emits and the prefix its source
names stop disagreeing. Not urgent -- the workflow accepts both -- but the two should not drift.

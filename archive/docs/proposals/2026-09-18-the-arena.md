# The arena: one learning loop, real money, nothing else on the board

Written September 18, 2026, 06:00-08:00 UTC, on the owner's brief: eight hours, cost no
constraint, "rapidly recursive self-improving trading agents that are exponentially profitable",
bold on architecture and on what the agents may risk, and a board that shows only what earns
its place.

## What the three-day record said

Every generation of chat desks lost money: eleven founders and their children, 2,082 decisions,
-$790 net over three days, -$2.95 of P&L per Sail dollar. The one thing that made money was
code: the pooled `kalshi_favorites` record (41 settled on 15 desks, 39 real) passed the evidence
gate at +2.6% per dollar. Nine self-founded families (gamelines, longtail, mentions, tennis,
gasoline, quarter-hour, sports-arb, ranges, weather) had between zero and forty decisions and no
evidence of anything. The Foundry's replays worked but its winners went to shadow desks, whose
modelled fills then had to prove the code again before it touched money; a candidate needed days
to reach the live book, and 104 rows on the site hid the six that mattered.

## The design

**The unit of learning is a strategy row on a live book, not a desk.** A live desk is its
venue's book: its sleeve is an equal share of the venue's equity, read again at every allocation
(`committee.venue_book`), so a deposit is capital at once and profit compounds. Every house
strategy of the family runs on it, and every Foundry candidate that beats the baselines is
installed on it as its own row at learning size (`foundry.deploy_live`, `explorers`). Each row
earns size on its own settled record and the family's pooled one (`Strategies.size_cap`), and a
row that loses after fees over `min_forward_settled` positions, or goes `forward_max_hours`
without fills, is retired (`Foundry.prune_explorers`). The loop is: the Foundry mutates code ->
replays it on real history -> the survivors trade real money small -> their settled records size
them up or retire them -> the records, the Firm Mind's rules and the research memory feed the
next mutation. No modelled fill stands between a candidate and its first real one.

**Chat sessions are the expensive way to lose.** Shadow desks run their strategy variants and
nothing else (`sessions.shadow_enabled` false); the live books keep two memo sessions a day
(`sessions.max_live_per_day`). The lab's playbook experiments and the nightly self-founding are
off: both bred chat desks.

**The board is the arena.** Nine families and 66 desks retired in one pass (a demotion first
for the one live weather desk); retired desks leave the checkpoint (the site's cap of 100 rows
had refused every checkpoint for ninety minutes). The floor page shows every strategy on a live
book with its record, its origin (house or Foundry) and the family's pooled evidence.

**More to read.** Eleven public hosts joined the floor's and the sandboxes' allowlists, with a
read-only module for each behind the strategy kit: Polymarket's odds on the questions Kalshi
lists (`kit.polymarket`, `kit.polymarket_matches`), open-meteo's GFS and ECMWF ensembles for the
temperature markets (`kit.ensemble`, `kit.bracket_probability`), perps funding across OKX,
Hyperliquid and Kraken with Deribit's implied vol (`kit.derivs`, `kit.basis`), ESPN's scoreboards
(`kit.scoreboard`, `kit.match_game`) and BLS prints with the Fed's calendar (`kit.macro`). Three
house strategies trade on them from tonight: `temps_ensemble`, `poly_cross`, `perp_funding`. They
have no history to replay, so the Foundry leaves them alone; their shadow variants and their
real records are their evidence.

**The owner's risk.** Learning size $40 on Kalshi and $60 on Coinbase; 35% of a live Kalshi
book in one position, 600 orders a day, 30% daily loss; 2x gross on Coinbase; the firm's event
caps 10% of the floor on one market and 25% on a cluster; the floor's daily loss limit 40%; the
size ramp starts at four settled and reaches full size at twice the gate's count; three real
settlements make a pooled record count.

## Addendum, 14:50 UTC: what the first morning taught

**Exits outrank caps.** The gateway's dollar caps were written for a VM that might go rogue;
they refused eighty exit orders while two shorts sat past their stops. An order the floor marks
as an exit now skips the dollar caps (the count cap still binds), and the caps themselves are
sized to a runaway loop, not a position: $2,000 an order, $60,000 a day.

**Evidence needs time, not turnover.** Forty-six explorers in seven hours, each replaced before
its first fill, is churn, not search. A live explorer is protected for six hours; when the book
is full the candidate earns a modelled record on a shadow desk instead. An explorer must also
expect to earn out of sample, not merely beat a losing baseline.

**Two lanes.** The Foundry is one lane per family, each with its own sandboxes and state, so the
Kalshi and the crypto search run at once.

**Two kinds of live book.** An explorers book (Mullins, Hilibrand) runs only the Foundry's
candidates; a house book (Mullins II, Mullins IV, Hilibrand III) runs the house strategies and
their promotions. The research loop's bets never spend the proven code's cash, attribution is
exact, and the venue-book allocation still gives every book an equal share of its venue.

**Every closed position is scored.** A short covered by a buy writes the same `desk.outcome` a
long sold does; the perps had no record at all until this morning.

**Same question, same clock.** A cross-venue match on a short-dated market must end within two
hours of its twin; Polymarket's noon BTC level is not Kalshi's 5pm one.
